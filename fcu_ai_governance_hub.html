1 Purpose and Institutional Alignment

Filamer Christian University, Inc. (FCU), guided by its vision of becoming a Christian University for Transformative Leadership, recognizes the transformative impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on teaching, learning, research, and institutional operations. This policy establishes the principles and standards for the responsible, ethical, secure, and effective use of AI to uphold academic integrity, protect human dignity, promote critical thinking, and foster innovation. It supports the University's Vision, Mission, and Goals and aligns with ISO 21001, the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), and applicable policies and issuances of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the National Privacy Commission (NPC).

2 Policy Principles

In guiding the integration of AI within the University, this policy operates on the following foundational assumptions adopted from the ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics:

2.1 Institution-wide Approach

AI shall be managed across the University in a coordinated manner, considering its impact on students, faculty, staff, institutional systems, and third-party service providers.

2.2 Statutory Compliance

This policy shall be implemented in accordance with applicable national laws, including the Data Privacy Act of 2012, issuances of the National Privacy Commission (NPC), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and other applicable regulations. Where conflicts arise, applicable laws shall prevail.

2.3 Responsible Innovation

Recognizing the rapid evolution of AI technologies, the University is committed to the responsible adoption of AI and the continuous improvement of its policies, practices, and institutional processes. This policy shall be reviewed periodically to ensure its continued relevance and alignment with technological developments, educational needs, and applicable laws and regulations.

3 Definition of Terms

To ensure clarity and uniformity across the academic community, the following definitions apply specifically within the context of the University:

3.1 System Category

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Computer-based systems that perform tasks requiring human intelligence, such as generating content, making predictions, analyzing information, or providing recommendations.

3.2 Generation Tools

Generative AI (GenAI)

Computer-based systems that perform tasks requiring human intelligence, such as generating content, making predictions, analyzing information, or providing recommendations.

3.3 Collaborative Work

AI-Assisted Work

Any academic, research, or administrative output created by a human author who utilized AI tools to support, enhance, or augment the process (e.g., brainstorming, outlining, language editing), provided that the human author remains responsible for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of the final output.

3.4 Academic Threshold

Major Assessment

An assessment contributing twenty percent (20%) or more of the final course grade or as determined by the instructor in the course syllabus.

4 Scope and Applicability

4.1 Academic & Administrative Levels: This policy applies to all departments, encompassing Basic Education, Undergraduate, and the Graduate School, ensuring a unified standard of digital ethics across all disciplines at FCU.

4.2 Operational Scope: This policy applies to all members of the University community who use, develop, procure, evaluate, or manage AI technologies for academic, research, administrative, or operational purposes.

5 Ethical Principles

5.1

Human Agency and Accountability

Human users remain ultimately responsible for all AI-assisted outputs. AI shall support, but not replace, informed human judgment. Decisions that significantly affect students or employees shall not rely solely on AI-generated outputs without appropriate human review.

5.2

Transparency and Explainability

Members of the University community shall disclose AI-assisted work whenever disclosure is required under this policy or by the instructor.

5.3

Data Privacy and Sovereignty

To protect the institution and its stakeholders, the unauthorized extraction or uploading of the university's intellectual property, proprietary teaching materials, and personal data into public or commercial AI models is strictly prohibited

 

5.4

Fairness, Equity, and Inclusivity

The university is committed to preventing a digital divide. No student shall be academically penalized for lacking access to premium, subscription-based AI tools. Furthermore, AI deployments must be regularly audited to ensure they do not systematically disadvantage any demographic or individuals with special educational needs.

 

5.5

Security

Institution-approved AI systems shall implement appropriate cybersecurity controls and be regularly maintained to reduce security risks and system failures.

 

6 AI Use in Teaching and Learning

The acceptable use of AI shall depend on the intended learning outcomes and the knowledge, skills, or competencies being assessed.

6.1.1 Strict Prohibition on Core Skill Replication

AI shall not be used when it replaces the specific knowledge, skills, or competencies being assessed. For example, Generative AI shall not be used to generate the core original content of research papers, personal reflection essays, or artistic assessments where the student's individual work is the primary basis of evaluation.

6.1.2 Allowable Enhancements:When permitted by the instructor and properly disclosed, AI may be used to create AI-Assisted Work to support learning through activities such as brainstorming, outlining, summarizing readings, improving readability, language editing, or other similar tasks that do not replace the intended learning outcomes.

6.2 Faculty Responsibilities:

6.2.1 Faculty members shall clearly communicate acceptable and unacceptable AI use in their course syllabi, assessment instructions, and other relevant learning materials.
6.2.2 Faculty members shall design assessments that appropriately evaluate higher-order thinking, authentic application of knowledge, and critical analysis, taking into consideration the capabilities and limitations of AI technologies.

6.3 Student Orientation:

6.3.1 At the beginning of each course or learning activity, faculty members shall orient students on the acceptable use of AI for course requirements, including any disclosure or citation requirements.
6.3.2 Students shall comply with the AI use requirements established by the instructor and this policy.

7 AI Use in Research Integrity

7.1.1 Authorship Limitations

AI systems can never be listed as authors or co-authors on research publications as non-human entities cannot assume legal or ethical liability.

7.1.2 Prohibition of Synthetic Data

Generating synthetic empirical data using GenAI to support research hypotheses constitutes severe data fabrication and research misconduct.

7.1.3 "Black Box" Liability

Researchers remain personally accountable for the accuracy, validity, originality, and citation integrity of all AI-assisted research outputs.

7.1.4 Research Ethics Oversight

Suspected algorithmic misrepresentation or departures from research standards will be reviewed by the Ethics Review Board under institutional misconduct protocols.

8 Data Privacy & Information Governance

8.1.1 For Students: Explicitly forbidden from uploading instructor copyrighted materials (exams, rubrics, lecture slides) into public AI platforms without documented authorization.

8.1.2 For Instructors: Educators are prohibited from uploading student intellectual property or personal data into third-party AI tools for grading without explicit, documented student consent.

8.1.3 Data Minimization: Only anonymized, strictly necessary operational data may be processed through approved AI systems.

8.3 Enterprise Procurement: The ICTSO and Data Privacy Officer (DPO) must conduct mandatory privacy and security audits on vendor software to guarantee zero model-training usage of institutional data.

9 Transparency & Mandatory Disclosure

9.1.1 & 9.1.2 Declaration for Major Assessments

For all major academic submissions (weighed at 20% or more of final grade), students MUST submit an AI Use Declaration Form. The declaration must explicitly name the AI model used and detail its exact functional application.

9.1.4 "AI Washing" Prohibition: Pursuant to NPC Advisory No. 2024-04, deceptive overstatement of AI capabilities or involvement is strictly banned.
9.1.5 Disciplinary Action: Failure to disclose required AI assistance constitutes academic misconduct subject to Student Handbook sanctions.

10 AI Misconduct, Investigation, and Appeals

10.1 Defined AI Misconduct Violations:

  • Submitting GenAI work without mandatory declaration.
  • Fabricating or falsifying research data/citations via AI.
  • Committing AI-generated plagiarism without attribution.
  • Submitting AI-generated content in whole or substantial part as original work.
  • Bypassing core competency evaluations using automated tools.

10.3 Safeguards Against False Positives in Investigations

Important: AI detection tools are known to produce false positives and shall NOT constitute sole proof of misconduct. All allegations must be corroborated by supporting evidence such as document version histories, oral defenses, writing style shifts, or draft progression checks under due process.

Institutional Document II • Faculty & Student Pedagogical Guide

Guide on AI Integration in Teaching and Learning

Filamer Christian University, Inc. · Practical Classroom Framework

Pedagogical Support Hub

Core Guiding Principles

  • • AI supports, but does not replace, active teaching and learning.
  • • Targeted learning outcomes remain the primary basis for all assessment.
  • • Students must continually demonstrate individual critical understanding.
  • • AI usage must be transparent, cited, and disclosed appropriately.

Determining Appropriate AI Use

Faculty decision questions before assigning coursework:

1. What specific knowledge or skill is being assessed?
2. Will AI assist learning, or will it complete the learning task for them?
3. Can students still independently demonstrate authentic mastery?

Standardized AI Use Categories

Category Description Permitted Examples
✔ Allowed AI may be used without restriction for the specified administrative or preliminary support task. Brainstorming, grammar checking, readability editing, outlining ideas.
⚠️ Allowed with Disclosure AI may be utilized, but students are required to submit formal acknowledgement detailing its application. Literature summaries, code generation, diagram drafting, section pre-drafting.
✖ Not Allowed AI use is strictly prohibited because it directly undermines essential intended learning outcomes. Exams, reflective journals, personal narratives, practical skill demonstrations.

Sample Syllabus Statements for Faculty

Instructors may copy and adapt these standardized clauses directly into course syllabi:

1. AI Allowed Statement

"Students may use AI tools for brainstorming, language improvement, and organizing ideas. Students remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of all submitted work."

2. Disclosure Statement

"Students may use AI as a learning aid for this assessment. Any significant AI assistance must be acknowledged in accordance with the University's Guidelines on AI Acknowledgment and Citation."

3. Prohibited Statement

"The use of AI is not permitted for this assessment because it would interfere with the intended learning outcomes. Submitted work must represent the student's own knowledge and understanding."

Good Practices to Adopt

  • ✔ Verify all AI-generated assertions and facts using peer-reviewed sources.
  • ✔ Critically evaluate logic, tone, and potential hallucination before using content.
  • ✔ Protect privacy by removing all personal and confidential university data.
  • ✔ Treat AI strictly as an aid to enhance, rather than replace, personal cognition.

Practices to Avoid

  • ✖ Submitting unedited AI-generated text as original intellectual work.
  • ✖ Utilizing AI to generate fake empirical data, citations, or references.
  • ✖ Using AI during prohibited assessments such as closed-book exams.
  • ✖ Uploading copyrighted exam questions or courseware into third-party AI platforms.
