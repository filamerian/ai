Core Philosophical Foundation

THE CENTRAL IDEA

"AI is a research assistant, not a replacement for the researcher."

AI CAN:

Processing & Pattern Recognition

  • Process large amounts of qualitative text rapidly
  • Identify recurring words, phrases, and linguistic patterns
  • Suggest preliminary codes and group related codes
  • Suggest initial categories and candidate themes
  • Summarize lengthy interview transcripts

RESEARCHER MUST:

Interpretation & Methodological Control

  • Understand context, participant experience, and culture
  • Interpret deeper meaning, irony, and subtle emotional cues
  • Apply rigorous methodological judgment and consider positionality
  • Validate all AI outputs against raw empirical transcripts
  • Make final analytical decisions and defend the research findings

Prompt Engineering for Research

AI CAN SUGGEST CODES — BUT CODES MUST BE VALIDATED

AI can suggest initial codes, categories, and themes, but researchers must always return to original raw transcripts to verify grounding.

Sample Methodological Prompt Constrained Input
"Using only this transcript, identify the recurring ideas and provide supporting excerpts. Do not interpret beyond the text."

WHY THIS PROMPT IS BETTER

  • Limits AI Scope: Strictly restricts the model to explicit provided textual evidence.
  • Reduces Hallucination: Minimizes unsupported speculations or ungrounded interpretations.
  • Demands Citation: Requires exact verbatim quotes for every suggested code.
  • Maintains Human Proximity: Forces the researcher to verify codes against raw participant data.

Socio-Linguistic Nuance

CONTEXT MATTERS

Highlighting multilingual and culturally contextualized research (e.g., Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Tagalog, English, and Code-switching).

LANGUAGE

Code-switching & local expressions.

AI often fails when participants mix Cebuano, Tagalog, and English in a single sentence, missing semantic shifts.
CULTURE

Shared communal norms.

Cultural concepts like "Pakikisama" or regional social dynamics cannot be interpreted by western-centric AI models without guidance.
IRONY

Sarcasm & non-literal text.

AI processes literal words; a participant saying "Oh, fantastic..." during distress is classified as positive sentiment by AI.
EMOTION

Subtext, hesitation, trauma.

Pauses, sighs, and emotional weight are erased in text processing, requiring human empathetic listening.
CONTEXT

Socio-political background.

Understanding why a participant withheld information requires knowing institutional or community dynamics.
EXPERIENCE

Lived human realities.

Phenomenological depth comes from researcher attunement, not word counting algorithms.

Ethical Data Handling

"Participant rights, consent, confidentiality, and research accountability are more important than AI convenience."

Warning: Do not assume that a public AI tool is automatically safe for research data. Unchecked data uploads can violate privacy laws and ethics approvals.

DATA MINIMIZATION PRINCIPLE

"IF AI DOES NOT NEED THE RAW DATA, DON'T GIVE IT THE RAW DATA."

1. ANONYMIZED DATA

Stripping all personal names, places, and direct identifying markers.

2. AGGREGATED DATA

Combining individual data points into group summaries or high-level totals.

3. SUMMARY DATA

Passing abstract structural outlines instead of raw transcript lines.

4. SYNTHETIC DATA

Using artificial representative examples to test prompts without real participant risk.

Consent to participate in research does not automatically mean permission to upload identifiable research data to any AI service.

Practical Application

PRIVACY-SAFE PROMPTING

See how raw unsafe prompts transform into sanitized, privacy-compliant AI requests.

UNSAFE PROMPT
"Here is my dataset containing student details and full transcripts from University X. Analyze it."

❌ Exposes confidential student information, institution names, and unredacted raw transcripts to cloud servers.

SAFER PROMPT
"Using this anonymized and aggregated dataset, identify the major patterns across Participant A through F."

✓ Redacts direct identifiers, aggregates responses, and utilizes pseudonyms.

PRIVACY-SAFE PROMPT CHECKLIST

Remove direct identifiers (names, IDs, emails)
Remove unnecessary indirect identifiers
Minimize data to essential excerpts only
Use summaries or synthetic data where possible
Confirm AI tool settings do not train on inputs
Governance & Compliance

CHECK THE POLICY BEFORE YOU USE AI

FUNDED RESEARCH

Always verify funding agency terms and grant agreements regarding third-party software and AI processing provisions.

UNIVERSITY RESEARCH

Review institutional AI guidelines, university data privacy charters, and thesis/capstone research office policies.

PUBLICATION

Check target journal publisher guidelines regarding AI disclosure statements, authorship criteria, and data transparency.

Methodological Comparison

QUANTITATIVE RESEARCH QUALITATIVE RESEARCH
Statistical computation & formula execution Qualitative text organization & grouping

Core Ethical Framework

THE 5 RESPONSIBLE AI PRINCIPLES

1. USE AI

Use AI where it genuinely assists research productivity.

2. CHECK AI

Rigorously validate and audit all AI outputs.

3. PROTECT PEOPLE

Protect privacy, consent, confidentiality, and dignity.

4. DOCUMENT AI

Record tools, prompts, and workflows transparently.

5. OWN DECISION

The researcher remains fully accountable for all work.
