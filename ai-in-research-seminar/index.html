AI Should Make Research Faster — Not Do Your Thinking.

Generative AI is changing how students, teachers, and researchers work. Tools like ChatGPT can brainstorm, organize, improve writing, analyze data, generate code, and explore directions. But the real question isn't "Can AI do this?" — it's "Should AI do this, and under what conditions?" AI should make research more efficient without taking away the researcher's thinking, responsibility, and intellectual ownership.

Core Principle 01

AI is a Research Assistant, Not the Researcher

Use AI to assist your thinking, not to outsource your thinking.

🤖

AI CAN

  • Assist & Suggest options
  • Organize & Summarize literature
  • Critique drafts & generate possibilities
  • Help process information & technical tasks
🧠

RESEARCHER MUST

  • Identify & decide the research problem
  • Design the study & decide data collection
  • Interpret findings & form conclusions
  • Verify all information & make final claims

"AI can help you do research, but AI should not become the researcher."

Risky Prompt 
"ChatGPT, write Chapter 2 of my thesis with citations."
Responsible Prompt 
"Based only on these five verified articles that I uploaded, help me identify possible themes for my literature review. Do not introduce sources that are not included in the documents."

The first prompt attempts to outsource a major intellectual task to AI. The second uses AI as a thinking and organizing assistant while the researcher remains fully responsible for the review.

Core Principle 02

Intellectual Ownership: Who Owns the Research?

Intellectual ownership means that the important ideas, decisions, reasoning, and scholarly judgments belong to the researcher. A key test: Can you explain why you made the decision?

Case Example: Cybersecurity Awareness Study

Suppose you are conducting a study about cybersecurity awareness among college students. You ask AI: "Suggest possible research problems related to cybersecurity awareness." AI gives you ten possibilities. You examine them, discuss them with your research group, compare them with existing literature, and eventually decide that phishing susceptibility is the most important problem.

→ You own the intellectual decision because AI provided suggestions, but you evaluated and selected the problem.

Speed vs Cognition

"AI may increase speed, but it should not decrease thinking."

Weak Approach (Outsourcing)
  1. Open ChatGPT.
  2. Ask for three problems.
  3. Copy the answers directly.
  4. Submit them without personal evaluation.

Result: AI does the thinking for you.

Strong Approach (Expanding)
  1. Think about personally observed problems in the university.
  2. Discuss possible problems with classmates and peers.
  3. Identify which problems are genuinely meaningful.
  4. Use AI to generate additional perspectives.
  5. Compare AI suggestions with original human ideas.
  6. Evaluate, select, and revise the final research problem.

Result: AI expands and refines the researcher's thinking.

Critical Warning

Fluency ≠ Truth

Generative AI can produce writing that sounds extremely intelligent, academic, and convincing. But good writing does not automatically mean correct information. An AI-generated paragraph can be grammatically flawless and confident in tone — and still be completely wrong.

⚠️

Understanding AI Hallucination

Hallucination occurs when the system generates information that appears factual but is actually incorrect, unsupported, or completely fabricated by the model.

Fabricated Citation Example:

"According to Santos and Reyes (2024), AI significantly improved research productivity among Filipino graduate students."

Questions to Ask Immediately:

1 Does the article actually exist?
2 Are Santos and Reyes real authors of that paper?
3 Does the journal actually exist?
4 Was it published in the stated year?
5 Does the paper say what AI claims it says?
6 Is the statement overgeneralized beyond the study?

"This is why researchers must verify, not merely trust."

Source Rigor

Never Stop at the AI-Generated Link

A common mistake: AI gives a citation → researcher clicks the link → researcher assumes the source is legitimate. That is not enough.

STEP 01

Check Existence

Search title & authors through academic databases or official publisher site.

STEP 02

Verify Journal

Confirm the journal is legitimate and the paper is actually published there.

STEP 03

Read Actual Article

Do not rely solely on the snippet, title, or AI summary.

STEP 04

Check Claims

Determine if the paper actually supports the statement you want to make.

STEP 05

Check Context

Ensure findings aren't wrongly generalized across populations.

"If a source cannot be verified, do not include it in the research."

Clicking Is Not Reading

Finding a source is not the same as understanding a source. Do not cite an article simply because Google found it, ChatGPT recommended it, the title looks relevant, or an AI tool summarized it.

AI Should Not Be Listed as an Author

Authorship involves responsibility and accountability. A human author can defend the paper, respond to reviewers, and address ethical concerns. An AI system cannot. Responsibility always remains with human authors.

Policy Spectrum

The AI Assistance Spectrum

When should AI assistance stop? Boundaries categorized by level of risk.

🟢 GREEN — Allowed (Ordinary Checking)

Tap to Expand

Description: AI may be used with ordinary checking.

Examples: Brainstorming · Generating possible ideas · Organizing information · Formatting assistance · Basic grammar corrections · Summarizing researcher-provided documents.

Note: Always depends on institutional, adviser, and course guidelines.

🟡 YELLOW — Allowed With Caution (Strong Verification Required)

Tap to Expand

Description: AI may be used, but rigorous human verification is required.

Examples: Literature synthesis · Statistical assistance · Interpretation support · Methodological suggestions · Qualitative analysis assistance · Code generation · Complex editing.

Note: The researcher must carefully check calculations, logic, and citations.

🔴 RED — Not Allowed (Ethics & Policy Violation)

Tap to Expand

Description: Violates research ethics, academic integrity, privacy, or institutional policy.

Examples: Writing entire thesis chapters for direct submission · Fabricating sources · Submitting AI findings without verification · Uploading confidential participant data · Letting AI make major research decisions.

Hard Boundaries

Four Situations Where AI Assistance Should Stop

AI can provide alternatives, but the researcher must make the final decision.

Appropriate: "Give me 3 approaches to analyze this problem with pros/cons."
Risky: "Choose the research design for my study and tell me what to do."

Uploading student records, names, personal health information, or confidential institutional records creates severe privacy and data protection violations.

Rule: Do not upload confidential research data unless institutional policy & protocols explicitly permit it.

Researchers should never include AI-generated statistical interpretations or complex text they cannot explain themselves.

Rule: If you cannot understand it, you should not claim it as your research finding.

AI text can become so overly polished that it no longer sounds like the researcher's authentic scholarly voice.

Self-Test: "Can I explain this paragraph without looking at it?" If no, revise it immediately.

Prompt Engineering

Responsible Prompting

Prompting is not simply asking AI a question. Good prompting sets clear constraints and boundaries.

WEAK: "Improve this." BETTER: Constrained Prompt 
"Improve the grammar of the paragraph below. Do not change the meaning, research claims, tense, or author's voice. Do not add new information. Do not introduce citations. Only correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, and awkward sentence construction."
WEAK: "Summarize these articles." BETTER: Source-Grounded Prompt 
"Summarize only the five uploaded articles. For each article, identify the research problem, participants, methodology, major findings, and limitations. Do not add information that is not contained in the uploaded documents."

Training AI to Maintain Your Personal Writing Style

Use direct steering feedback to keep your academic voice:

💬 "That is not how I write." 💬 "Do not use that terminology." 💬 "You changed my meaning." 💬 "Keep my original voice."

Institutional Context

Why Policy & Context Matter

A blanket policy saying "Use AI responsibly" is too vague. Guidance must be specific.

A Useful Policy Must Explain:

  • What AI use is permitted, restricted, or prohibited
  • Data privacy standards for uploaded files
  • Disclosure and documentation requirements
  • Specific rules for students, faculty, and researchers

Classroom Context & Assessment:

AI ethics is context-dependent. Editing grammar might be fine in an IT course but prohibited in an English writing class where writing itself is being assessed.

Agentic AI & Advanced Tools

Agentic AI tools perform multi-step workflows (like literature extraction or statistical coding). As tools grow more autonomous, the researcher's role shifts toward methodological validation, quality control, and ethical oversight.

A quick recap before you go.

"Let AI make your research faster, but never let it make you think less."

The core message from Dr. Dave E. Marcial's masterclass is clear: responsible AI use is not about choosing between "AI" and "no AI" — it's about placing AI in its rightful supporting position. The goal is not to produce research that merely looks intelligent, but to produce research you genuinely understand, ethically stand behind, and can confidently defend.
