Core Concept

What Is Session 3 About?

"The central message of Session 3 is that Generative AI can assist researchers throughout the data-analysis process, but it should function as an assistant rather than a replacement for the researcher, statistician, or research adviser."

01

AI-Assisted Data Analysis

Leveraging LLMs to streamline routine statistical tasks, inspect variables, and generate code.

AI helps structure datasets, calculate basic statistics, and write syntax for R, Python, or SPSS while preserving human oversight.

02

Data Visualization

Recommending visually compelling, accurate graphical representations for research questions.

AI assists in choosing histograms, scatter plots, or box plots that accurately reflect distribution without visual deception.

03

Statistical Interpretation

Translating complex statistical outputs into clear, context-aware scholarly narratives.

AI provides technical breakdowns and plain-language summaries that researchers contextualize within literature.

04

Human Validation

Maintaining mandatory human verification over calculations, assumptions, and claims.

Every automated output must be independently checked by researchers or statisticians for accuracy and logic.

05

Research Ethics

Preventing data fabrication, citation hallucination, and selective reporting.

Strictly avoiding p-hacking, artificial result generation, or submitting AI unverified content as personal scholarly work.

06

Data Privacy

Safeguarding participant datasets from unauthorized AI training uploads.

Ensuring complete anonymization and institutional compliance before introducing datasets to public AI models.

Core Philosophy

THE BIG IDEA

"AI IS AN ASSISTANT, NOT THE RESEARCHER."

AI can help inspect, summarize, analyze, visualize, explain, interpret, and improve research reporting. However, the researcher remains responsible for methodological decisions, interpretation, conclusions, ethical judgment, and validation.

AI Role

Automated Execution & Drafting
  • Assists with data structuring & syntax
  • Suggests statistical test options
  • Computes descriptive and inferential math
  • Explains raw code & numeric outputs
  • Visualizes distributions and charts
  • Drafts initial wording & summaries

Researcher Role

Critical Authority & Ownership
  • Decides methodology & study design
  • Validates assumptions & calculation accuracy
  • Interprets results within domain context
  • Contextualizes findings with literature
  • Defends claims during defense & peer review
  • Takes Responsibility for final paper

01 / UNDERSTAND

Understand Data Context

Processes metadata, identifies variable types, and evaluates measurement levels.

  • Identify independent & dependent variables
  • Classify nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio levels
  • Map survey Likert scales automatically
02 / INSPECT

Inspect Quality & Anomaly

Scans datasets for missing parameters, duplicates, and impossible values.

  • Flag blank cells & non-standard missing codes
  • Detect duplicated response rows
  • Highlight extreme statistical outliers
03 / SUMMARIZE

Summarize Distributions

Computes descriptive statistics, central tendency, and dispersion metrics.

  • Means, medians, modes, standard deviations
  • Frequencies and demographic percentages
  • Skewness and kurtosis initial checks
04 / ANALYZE

Analyze Procedures

Executes or scripts t-tests, ANOVA, correlations, and regression models.

  • Group differences (ANOVA / Independent t-test)
  • Pearson r & Spearman rank correlations
  • Multiple linear & logistic regressions
05 / VISUALIZE

Visualize Insights

Recommends visual chart formats and generates code for publication graphics.

  • Scatter plots with trendlines
  • Box plots for group spread comparisons
  • High-resolution APA-styled graphics
06 / EXPLAIN

Explain Statistical Output

Translates complex math, p-values, and effect sizes into clear English.

  • Explains p-value threshold significance
  • Converts confidence intervals into plain language
  • Outlines statistical limitations clearly

Prompt Engineering Strategy

PROMPT CHAINING

Instead of asking AI to analyze everything in one prompt, guide the AI through a structured sequence of research-specific questions.

CORRELATION ≠ CAUSATION

A statistically significant relationship does not automatically mean that one variable causes another. AI might describe strong mathematical co-occurrence, but researchers must avoid making unsupported causal leaps.

Valid Scientific Claim

"Variable X and Variable Y exhibit a significant correlation (r = .68, p < .01)."

Unsupported Causal Claim CLICK TO TEST

"Variable X directly causes Variable Y to increase."

METHODOLOGICAL WARNING TRIPPED!

Unless your study uses a randomized experimental design with controlled confounding variables, claiming causality from observational correlation is a fatal methodological error!

Expert Collaboration

DOES AI REPLACE THE STATISTICIAN?

NO.

AI may reduce some routine computational work, but statistical expertise remains crucial for methodological decisions, validation, assumption checking, interpretation, and defensible conclusions.

GenAI Capabilities

  • Fast computation & syntax generation
  • Conversational explanations of formulas
  • Immediate 24/7 availability for drafting
  • Broad preliminary chart recommendations

Statistician Expertise

  • Deep methodological domain judgment
  • Contextual assumption & normality verification
  • Study design, sampling, & power calculations
  • Professional accountability & thesis defense backing
"AI can change the role of the statistician—but it does not eliminate the need for statistical expertise."
Research Integrity

ETHICAL VS. UNETHICAL AI USE

RESPONSIBLE USE

  • Improve writing clarity & grammar
  • Explain complex statistical formulas
  • Suggest analytical approaches for consideration
  • Assist with chart visualization styling
  • Identify possible data-quality anomalies
  • Help organize researcher-provided information

IRRESPONSIBLE USE

  • Manufacture or fabricate survey results
  • Manipulate data to make nonsignificant findings significant
  • Invent methodology that was never conducted
  • Claim causation from correlation without proof
  • Ignore limitations or cherry-pick favorable data
  • Submit unverified AI output as original thesis work
Participant Rights

PROTECT YOUR RESEARCH PARTICIPANTS

Mandatory Anonymization Rule

Do not upload identifiable research data directly into cloud AI systems. Removing only participant names is often insufficient; combinations of age, address, and phone number can lead to re-identification.
