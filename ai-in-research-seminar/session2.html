PART I • PROBLEM CONCEPTUALIZATION

From Idea to Researchable Problem

The conceptualization pathway is: Identification → Context → Scope → Research Question → Hypothesis.

01

Identify

Recognize challenges, gaps, and underexplored areas.

02

Understand Context

Examine background, trends, stakeholders, and setting.

03

Define Scope

Move from broad topics to manageable, researchable problems.

04

Frame Questions

Generate possible qualitative or quantitative questions.

05

Formulate

Develop or refine hypotheses when the design requires them.

TOO BROAD

AI in rural education

A starting area, but too wide to function well as a research problem.

NARROWER

AI tools for teacher training in rural schools

A more focused direction that can be further bounded by population, location, period, or discipline.

Prompt Design

Context Makes Prompts More Useful

Instead of giving only a generic role, provide relevant context such as discipline, research interests, expertise, setting, population, and the purpose of the brainstorming task.

I am a researcher working in [discipline] with an interest in [topic].
My research context is [population/location/setting].
I am brainstorming possible research problems, not asking for a finished study.
Suggest five potential problems worth investigating.
Do not invent evidence, studies, or citations.
I will evaluate and select the most relevant possibilities.
Ethical Prompting

Avoid Fabrication, Bias, and Forced Conclusions

UNSAFE PATTERN

Ask AI to manufacture support

"Create a list of studies supporting that AI always improves learning outcomes."

This begins with a predetermined conclusion and can encourage unsupported or fabricated evidence.

RESPONSIBLE PATTERN

Ask for evidence neutrally

"Summarize findings from peer-reviewed research on AI's impact on learning outcomes. Identify agreements, disagreements, and limitations."

The prompt allows evidence to reveal what the literature actually says.

Do not use AI to confirm what you already want to believe.

Ask questions that allow evidence to reveal what is actually happening.

Summarization vs Synthesis

Know the Difference

SUMMARIZATION

Understand Individual Studies

  • Methods
  • Main findings
  • Purpose
  • Study-specific limitations
SYNTHESIS

Connect Studies

  • Agreements and disagreements
  • Methodological differences
  • Common themes
  • Research gaps
  • Strengths and limitations
BETTER LITERATURE WRITING

Instead of: “Study A found… Study B found… Study C found…”

Compare: “Studies A and B generally agree that…, while Study C reports a different result because…”

Verification

Never Stop at the AI Answer

RELEVANCE

Does the source actually address the research topic?

ACCURACY

Can the claim be traced to the original study?

COHERENCE

Does the information logically fit the research discussion?

  1. 1. Locate the original source.
  2. 2. Check that the source exists.
  3. 3. Examine the original content.
  4. 4. Verify the AI's claim.
  5. 5. Check source appropriateness.
  6. 6. Confirm target-journal requirements.
Full-text principle: Whenever possible, examine the actual paper rather than relying only on an AI-generated description or abstract.
Boundaries & Transparency

Policy Is Part of Research Practice

Policies can differ

  • Some settings prohibit AI in certain stages.
  • Some allow literature searching.
  • Some allow synthesis or editing.
  • Some require disclosure.

When disclosure is required

  • Where AI was used
  • What it was used for
  • Whether it generated or augmented content
  • What the researcher contributed
  • How output was verified

Do not assume one AI rule applies everywhere. Check the specific institutional, course, funder, and journal requirements.

Key Takeaways

What to Remember

01 Begin with your own research interest and intellectual direction.

02 Use AI to identify, clarify, narrow, and augment—not to replace conceptual thinking.

03 Search prompts become stronger when source, context, population, and time boundaries are explicit.

04 A literature review should compare and synthesize studies, not merely list them.

05 Verify AI-generated claims against original sources.

06 Full-text access and legitimate source access matter.

07 Relevance, accuracy, and coherence are practical checks for AI-supported literature work.

08 Follow the applicable institutional, course, funder, and journal policies.

09 When required, transparently declare how AI was used.

10 AI should make research more efficient without removing the researcher from the process.

Questions for Reflection

Think Beyond the Prompt

1. What part of your research problem must originate from your own thinking?
2. How could a leading prompt distort your literature search?
3. What evidence would you need before trusting an AI-generated citation?
4. How is synthesis different from summarizing several papers?
5. What boundaries should your class or institution establish for AI-assisted research?
6. If AI suggested five research problems, what criteria would you use to select one?
Glossary

Key Terms

Problem conceptualization

The process of developing and refining a research problem before detailed investigation.

Research gap

An area where existing knowledge or evidence is incomplete, limited, inconsistent, or underexplored.

Literature matrix

A structured table used to organize studies by themes, variables, methods, or other research criteria.

Evidence synthesis

The process of connecting evidence across studies to identify patterns, differences, gaps, and implications.

Hallucination

AI output that presents unsupported or incorrect information as though it were factual.

Confirmation bias

The tendency to favor information that supports an existing belief while overlooking contrary evidence.
