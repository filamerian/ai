SESSION 01 • RESEARCH & AI

Responsible Generative AI Use in Research

A practical learning module on intellectual ownership, responsible prompting, verification, privacy, academic integrity, and human judgment.

Topic Overview

AI Should Assist the Researcher

Generative AI can support many stages of research, but the researcher remains responsible for the thinking, decisions, verification, and final claims.

01 • OWNERSHIP

Keep the Thinking Human

The researcher should retain ownership of the research problem, interpretation, decisions, and conclusions.

02 • VERIFICATION

Do Not Trust Fluency

AI output can contain errors, unsupported claims, or fabricated references. Important information must be checked.

03 • RESPONSIBILITY

Human Accountability

The person submitting the work remains responsible for its accuracy, integrity, privacy, and compliance with applicable rules.

"AI can help you do research, but AI should not become the researcher."

Responsible Use Framework

Purpose → Process → Prompt Quality → Post-Check

A simple decision structure for keeping AI use human-centered.

STEP 01

Purpose

Why am I using AI? Learning, brainstorming, feedback, organization—or replacing the task?

STEP 02

Process

What cognitive work, analysis, interpretation, and decisions must remain mine?

STEP 03

Prompt Quality

Is the request specific, ethical, honest, contextualized, and appropriately constrained?

STEP 04

Post-Check

Verify sources and claims, revise the output, reflect, and make the final judgment.

Prompt Engineering

Responsible Prompting

Good prompts define clear boundaries instead of asking AI to perform the entire intellectual task.

WEAK: "Improve this." BETTER: Constrained Prompt 
"Improve the grammar of the paragraph below. Do not change the meaning, research claims, tense, or author's voice. Do not add new information. Only correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, and awkward sentence construction."
WEAK: "Write my literature review." BETTER: Source-Grounded Prompt 
"Based only on the verified articles I uploaded, help me identify recurring themes. Do not introduce sources that are not in the documents. I will decide which themes belong in my literature review."
Human Control Boundaries

When Should AI Stop?

AI can help identify possibilities, but the researcher should retain the final decision and be able to explain the reasoning.
Check institutional, research, and data-privacy requirements before entering sensitive information into an external AI service.
If you cannot explain or verify an important AI-generated claim, do not present it as your research finding.
Rule: If you cannot understand it, you should not claim it as your research finding.
AI text can become so polished that it no longer represents the researcher's authentic scholarly voice.
Self-Test: "Can I explain this paragraph without looking at it?" If no, revise it.
Lifecycle Support

AI Across the Research Lifecycle

AI can support many research activities when use remains purposeful, verifiable, and policy-compliant.

STAGE 01

Conceptualization

Explore possible research problems and angles.

STAGE 02

Literature Review

Organize and work with verified source material.

STAGE 03

Hypothesis Generation

Explore possible relationships and questions.

STAGE 04

Data Work

Support analysis while retaining human interpretation.

STAGE 05

Writing

Assist with organization and refinement where permitted.

STAGE 06

Coding

Generate or explain code while checking correctness.

STAGE 07

Interpretation

Interpret findings and form conclusions yourself.

STAGE 08

Verification & Automation

Verify information and reduce repetitive mechanical work.

"The objective is to reduce time spent on low-level repetitive work so researchers can spend more time on high-level thinking."

Use Spectrum

Generally Acceptable → Caution → Avoid

Supportive
  • Brainstorming
  • Concept clarification
  • Examples
  • Feedback
  • Organization
High Caution
  • Literature synthesis
  • Data analysis
  • Research writing
  • Complex interpretation
  • Anything requiring strong verification
Problematic
  • Replacing the researcher's thinking
  • Fabricated or unverified sources
  • Deceptive use
  • Prohibited confidential data
  • Final work where AI use is prohibited
